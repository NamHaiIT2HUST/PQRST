\begin{abstract}
Transfer Entropy (TE) is a widely used model-free measure for discovering directed causal interactions in complex dynamical systems. However, its practical translation to continuous wearable sensing and clinical edge computing is obstructed by three foundational bottlenecks: (1) ensuring statistical stationarity in non-stationary biosignals demands extracting ultra-short temporal windows ($N \le 100$ samples), a sparse data regime where traditional non-parametric estimators (e.g., Kraskov-St{\"o}gbauer-Grassberger, KSG) suffer from catastrophic finite-sample variance explosion due to the curse of dimensionality, while recent deep generative models (e.g., Transformer-based TREET, Diffusion-based TENDE) incur prohibitive computational latency ($\sim 20$ minutes per pair on high-end GPUs) or severe sample-starvation; (2) neural estimators trained on synthetic distributions inevitably encounter severe latent covariate shift when deployed on empirical biosignals, yielding inverted causal directions; and (3) biological recording channels are corrupted by persistent additive noise. To overcome these barriers, we propose an Amortized Neural Transfer Entropy framework designed explicitly for the short-sample edge-computing regime. We theoretically prove four foundational propositions: (i) non-separability of statistics networks; (ii) exact variance reduction achieved via joint parameter sharing and unified marginal permutations; (iii) mathematical robustness against additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN); and (iv) an analytical variance crossover boundary $N^* \in [30, 50]$ where amortized representations strictly outperform $k$-NN estimators. To bridge the reality gap without ground-truth causal labels, we formulate an out-of-fold Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) protocol optimizing the Donsker-Varadhan variational objective. We systematically benchmark five diverse model architectures ranging from a 369-parameter lightweight MLP (achieving 7.0~ms per-window inference latency) to a hybrid classical-quantum circuit (MLP + 4-qubit VQC). Finally, validation on the complete 40-subject PhysioNet Fantasia database (20 Young vs. 20 Old, 9,443 physiological windows) confirms statistically significant age-related cardiorespiratory decoupling ($p = 0.0045$, Cohen's $d = 0.67$) while achieving a 2.2-fold variance reduction over classical KSG, resolving a longstanding algorithmic selection bias in earlier literature.
\end{abstract}
