\section{Conclusion}
In this paper, we introduced an Amortized Neural Transfer Entropy framework designed explicitly to address the fundamental small-sample and domain-shift challenges inherent in biomedical time series. Grounded in four mathematical propositions, we proved that parameter sharing within a unified masked statistics network induces positive covariance between joint and marginal estimates, strictly eliminating Monte Carlo estimation variance. Furthermore, we established an analytical variance crossover boundary $N^* \in [30, 50]$ where amortized representations strictly outperform classical $k$-NN estimators, and proved quadratic noise damping under additive Gaussian perturbations.

To bridge the reality gap without ground-truth causal labels, our unsupervised test-time adaptation protocol exploits the variational Donsker-Varadhan bound, projecting empirical physiological recordings onto the valid latent manifold. Comprehensive benchmarking across five model families demonstrated that a 369-parameter Small MLP delivers 7.0~ms inference latency with accuracy matching deep networks, while a 4-qubit Hybrid Classical-Quantum circuit achieved unprecedented variance reduction ($1.77 \times 10^{-5}$). Finally, validation across all 40 subjects in the PhysioNet Fantasia database confirmed significant age-related cardiorespiratory decoupling ($p = 0.0045$, Cohen's $d = 0.67$) with a 2.2-fold variance reduction over KSG, resolving a longstanding algorithmic selection bias.

Future work will focus on deploying the lightweight statistics network directly onto ARM Cortex-M microcontrollers for real-time edge processing and evaluating hybrid quantum variational circuits on physical quantum processing units (QPUs) for high-dimensional, multi-organ causal network discovery.
